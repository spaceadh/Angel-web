Google is changing how people find website links inside its AI search answers. For businesses that depend on search, the practical question is whether those links bring visitors who go on to enquire, book, or buy.

In its [15 October 2026 announcement](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/updates-ai-mode-overviews-explore-web/), Google described more prominent links across AI Mode and AI Overviews. The update gives website owners something worth monitoring, although the announcement provides no measured traffic gain for a typical business.

## What Google announced

Google says it is increasing the presence of links embedded within AI responses and displaying them higher in answers more frequently. It is also making some related-article links more prominent, with image thumbnails.

Other changes have different statuses. A section highlighting official websites for specific tasks is a test. Timely-article carousels are expanding to additional locations and languages. The announcement does not confirm that every change is available to every user in Kenya.

That distinction matters when checking your own searches. An interface you see on one device is an observation of that experience, rather than evidence of a complete rollout.

## Better placement still needs a reason to click

Independent research offers useful context. In [Pew Research Center’s July 2025 analysis](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/), visits to search pages with an AI summary led to a traditional-result click 8% of the time, compared with 15% for pages without one. Clicks on links within the AI summary occurred in 1% of visits with a summary.

Pew examined browsing activity from 900 US adults in March 2025 and collected corresponding search results in April. The study is observational: it does not prove that an AI summary caused every difference in clicking. It also cannot tell us how Kenyan customers behave today.

Our reading is that visibility and visits should be measured separately. Being mentioned in an answer can be useful, but a business still needs to know what happens after someone sees its name.

## What should a business website offer?

Start with information that helps a visitor make a decision. A general definition may answer a basic question completely. A detailed service page can help someone assess whether your business is suitable for their particular job.

For a Kenyan website project, useful information might include what a quoted scope covers, how content is prepared, which integrations are supported, and how maintenance works. For a business serving international customers, clarify delivery locations, currencies, and support arrangements where relevant.

Original photographs, worked examples, and honest explanations of limitations can make that information easier to assess. Use real evidence you are permitted to share. A fabricated case study weakens the very credibility the page is supposed to establish.

## Keep the technical work grounded

Google’s [website guidance for AI search features](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) says supporting pages must be indexed and eligible to appear with a search snippet. It says special AI markup is unnecessary, and eligibility does not guarantee inclusion.

Its [generative search optimisation guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) continues to emphasise SEO and content with an original contribution. That supports a practical approach: check that important pages are accessible, then improve what those pages help a customer understand.

Before buying an “AI visibility” package, ask what will change on your site, how success will be measured, and which claims can be demonstrated. Treat guaranteed placement claims cautiously.

## A useful next step this week

Choose one commercially important page and review it against actual customer questions. Add missing information, give useful images descriptive alternative text, and test the enquiry process on mobile.

Record changes and compare relevant traffic with qualified enquiries over time. Keep occasional search screenshots as examples of what appeared, with dates and context. They are useful observations, but they cannot represent every user’s results.

### Should Kenyan businesses pay attention to this global update?

Yes, where Google search contributes to customer discovery. Google announced [AI Mode’s African expansion in August 2025](https://blog.google/intl/en-africa/products/explore-get-answers/google-search-introducing-ai-mode-in-africa/). That regional context makes the development relevant; it does not establish local availability of each newly announced feature.
