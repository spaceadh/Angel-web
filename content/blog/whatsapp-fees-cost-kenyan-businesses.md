This month, the tools Kenyan businesses use to win customers started costing more in small but real ways. WhatsApp now charges for replies, Meta wants your ad clicks to stay inside Facebook and Instagram, and AI features are turning into a separate bill. Here's what each change costs you, and what to do about it this week.

## How much does WhatsApp cost a Kenyan business now?

**About KSh 0.52 per reply, after 1,000 free replies a month.** Since 1 October, Meta charges businesses each time they reply to a customer on the WhatsApp Business Platform, the version of WhatsApp that runs through CRMs, chatbots and shared inboxes. Replies used to be free. Each business number still gets 1,000 free replies every month, and unused ones don't carry over.

If you chat with customers yourself in the free WhatsApp Business app on your phone, these per-message charges don't apply to you.

Here's what replies now cost at Meta's price for Kenya, before any fees your software provider adds:

| Customer replies a month | What Meta charges (approx.) |
| ------------------------ | --------------------------- |
| Up to 1,000              | Free                        |
| 5,000                    | KSh 2,080                   |
| 20,000                   | KSh 9,870                   |
| 50,000                   | KSh 25,440                  |

For most small businesses, that's manageable. The bigger cost hides in automation.

Meta's own AI assistant for businesses is billed separately, at about 4 to 5 US cents a message by Meta's estimate. That's 1,000 AI replies for roughly KSh 5,200 to KSh 6,500, before any of them become a sale.

**What to do:**

- **Ask your provider** whether your WhatsApp runs through Meta's business platform, and how many replies you send a month.
- **Use AI replies where they save real staff time**, such as answering the same five questions every day, and let your team handle sales conversations.
- **Start conversations from Click-to-WhatsApp ads.** When a customer taps your ad on their phone and messages you, Meta opens a free window that can last up to 7 days for normal replies.

## Will your Facebook and Instagram ads stop sending people to your website?

**Possibly, from 28 October, unless you check one setting.** Meta has told advertisers it will switch on "product browsing" for ads that are already running, according to the trade publications PPC Land and MediaPost. With it on, a customer who taps your ad may see your product on a page inside Facebook or Instagram instead of landing on your website. Meta says its August test with about 117,700 advertisers produced around 3% more conversions on average. It hadn't published an official help page on the change when we checked.

**What it means for you:** fewer people reach your website, so you collect fewer email sign-ups, have less data in Google Analytics and build smaller audiences to retarget. For some shops, selling inside Instagram may still work well. The point is to choose, not to let Meta choose for you.

**What to do:** open Ads Manager before 28 October, look for "product browsing" in your ad's creative and destination settings, and turn it off for any ad that should send people to your site.

## Why are the big marketing companies merging email and WhatsApp?

**Because customers move between channels, and businesses want one view of them.** On 20 October, Salesforce agreed to buy Braze for about US$5.66 billion. Braze helps about 2,770 companies message customers by email, text, app and WhatsApp from one place. The deal still needs approval from Braze shareholders and regulators.

You don't need a billion-dollar platform to copy the idea. A Kenyan business can get most of the benefit with one customer list for email and WhatsApp. Feed it from your website forms and ads, then follow up on the channel each customer actually answers.

**Watch your AI bill, too.** AI features are becoming paid extras. On 15 October, HubSpot switched on AI spending caps for its customers by default. Email platform Klaviyo made its AI marketing assistant available to all customers on 13 October. These tools charge in US dollars, so set limits and check usage monthly.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is WhatsApp Business free in Kenya?

The WhatsApp Business app is free to use on your phone. Businesses that send messages through software, such as a CRM, chatbot or shared inbox, pay Meta per message. That's about KSh 0.52 per reply after 1,000 free replies a month, and about KSh 2.92 per promotional message.

### How many free WhatsApp business messages do you get?

Each business number gets 1,000 free customer replies a month on Meta's business platform. Unused replies don't roll over to the next month.

### Do Click-to-WhatsApp ads make messages free?

Yes, for a while. When a customer starts a chat by tapping your ad on their phone, Meta opens a free window that can last up to 7 days for normal replies and promotional messages. Messages from Meta's AI assistant are still charged.
