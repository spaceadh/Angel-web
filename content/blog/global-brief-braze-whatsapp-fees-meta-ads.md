Two forces shaped this week in marketing tech. The big platforms want to keep your customer's click inside their own apps, and they are starting to put a price on every AI-powered message and task. For Kenyan businesses that sell through WhatsApp, Instagram and a website, both trends cost money unless you act.

**The week at a glance**

- **Salesforce** agreed to buy customer-messaging platform **Braze** for about $5.66 billion.
- **WhatsApp's** new charges for business replies are in their first month. In Kenya, each reply beyond the free 1,000 a month costs about KSh 0.52.
- **Meta** will reportedly switch on in-app "product browsing" for existing Facebook and Instagram ads on 28 October unless advertisers opt out.
- **HubSpot, Zapier and Klaviyo** all moved on AI agents, and how you pay for them.
- **Patch week:** critical fixes landed for **Next.js**, **WordPress** and **n8n**.
- **Africa:** Microsoft is cutting its Nairobi engineering hub, while Meta backed Kenyan AI start-ups.

_Shilling figures use the CBK rate of KSh 129.81 to the US dollar on 15 October 2026._

## 1. Salesforce buys Braze: the email race becomes an all-channel race

**What changed.** On 20 October, Salesforce signed a definitive agreement to buy Braze for about $5.66 billion in enterprise value, or $47.50 in cash per share. Braze is a customer engagement platform used by about 2,770 companies. It reaches customers across "mobile apps, websites, email, SMS, WhatsApp, and more", in Salesforce's words. The deal is expected to close in Salesforce's 2028 financial year, subject to Braze shareholder and regulatory approval. _Status: announced, not closed._

**What it means for Kenyan businesses.** Our view: few Kenyan SMEs use Braze or Salesforce Marketing Cloud directly, but the deal shows where email marketing is heading. The biggest players no longer sell "email tools". They sell one system that decides whether to reach a customer by email, SMS or WhatsApp, and when. Banks, telcos and fintechs already on Salesforce may see Braze offered as a bundle. For smaller businesses, the lesson is the same at a smaller scale: keep your email list and your WhatsApp contacts in one place, so you can follow up on the channel each customer actually uses.

## 2. WhatsApp replies now cost money: Kenya's rates

**What changed.** Since 1 October, Meta charges businesses on the WhatsApp Business Platform for "service" messages, meaning replies to a customer who wrote first. Utility messages sent during an open chat, such as order updates, are now charged too. Both had been free. Each business number gets **1,000 free service messages a month**, and unused ones don't roll over. Conversations a customer starts from a Click-to-WhatsApp ad on their phone open a free window that can last up to 7 days, though AI agent messages are still charged. Meta's own AI agent for business is billed separately at $2 per million tokens, which Meta says works out to about 4–5 US cents a message. _Status: in effect since 1 October._

Kenya falls under Meta's "Rest of Africa" pricing group.

**What it means for Kenyan businesses.** Our view: if you use the free WhatsApp Business app on your phone, nothing changes. If a CRM, chatbot or helpdesk sends your WhatsApp messages through the API, check your volumes. A shop answering 5,000 customer questions a month pays for 4,000 of them, about KSh 2,080 at Meta's rate before any provider fees. That's affordable. The bigger cost is Meta's AI agent, at 10 to 12 times the price of a standard reply. Use it where it clearly saves staff time, and start conversations from Click-to-WhatsApp ads, which can open a free window of up to 7 days for standard messages. ## 3. Meta's 28 October switch: your ads may stop sending people to your site

**What changed.** Meta has told advertisers it will turn on "product browsing" for ads that are already running from 28 October, unless they opt out, according to PPC Land and MediaPost. With product browsing on, a tap on an ad can open a product page inside Facebook or Instagram instead of your website. Meta's own test of about 117,700 advertisers in August reported around 3% more conversions on average, but it didn't define which conversions it counted. Meta had not published an official help article on the change when we checked. _Status: reported, effective 28 October._

**What it means for Kenyan businesses.** Our view: an extra in-app step means fewer visits to your own site. That can shrink the audiences you retarget, the email sign-ups you capture and the data you see in Google Analytics. It may still suit some shops. Decide on purpose: check the setting in Ads Manager under Advantage+ creative and destination settings, and opt out of the notice before 28 October if you want clicks to land on your website.

## 4. AI agents get a price tag

AI features are moving from free extras to metered line items. Three platforms Kenyan marketers use made changes this month.

- **HubSpot** (15 October) switched on AI spending caps by default. Each account's limit is set up to 50% above its monthly credits. Customers can also cap their marketing-contacts tier, swap unused seats for credits mid-contract, and pay for extra usage as they go.
- **Zapier** has folded its AI agents into ordinary Zaps as "AI by Zapier". Each AI step now uses 1, 3 or 5 tasks depending on the model, and a single run that passes 75 tasks pauses for a human to check.
- **Klaviyo** (13 October) made Composer, its AI marketing agent, available to all customers. It answers plain-language questions about your data and compares results with benchmarks from more than 205,000 Klaviyo customers. Klaviyo's announcement doesn't mention extra charges.

**What it means for Kenyan businesses.** Our view: these tools bill in US dollars, so an AI feature left running can quietly add to costs when you're already exposed to the exchange rate. This week, check the spending cap in HubSpot, set a task limit on AI steps in Zapier, and look at which model each step uses. Cheaper models handle simple sorting and tagging well.

## 6. Africa watch: Microsoft steps back, Meta funds local AI

**Microsoft's Nairobi hub.** Staff at Microsoft's Africa Development Centre in Nairobi were told on 12 October that the engineering centre is closing, according to employees who spoke to CIO Africa. Microsoft called them "proposed changes" within global restructuring and said it remains committed to Kenya. It hasn't said how many jobs are affected. The centre was announced in 2019 with a planned $100 million over five years and opened in March 2022. Microsoft closed its Lagos centre in 2024. ICT Cabinet Secretary William Kabogo met a Microsoft delegation the same day and said both sides would draw up a joint plan on AI, cybersecurity and digital skills.

**Meta's Kenyan AI start-ups.** Meta and Nairobi accelerator This Is Digital held the first AI Academy Kenya pitchathon, picking 10 finalists from more than 400 applications. Neural Labs Africa and Me-Nasoma each won $5,000 in equity-free funding plus Meta ad credits. Finalists included Sawazisha AI, a WhatsApp assistant for KRA eTIMS invoicing, and TemboTrade, a sales, inventory and M-Pesa payments platform for small businesses.

**Our view:** global tech firms will keep reshuffling their African hubs, but useful AI for Kenyan businesses is increasingly being built locally, on the tools people already use: WhatsApp, M-Pesa and eTIMS. Experienced engineers entering the local market may also make it easier for Kenyan firms to hire.
